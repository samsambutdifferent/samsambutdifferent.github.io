Discovery of a Small Molecule Insulin Mimetic with Antidiabetic Activity in Mice

Zhang B, Salituro G, Szalkowski D, Li Z, Zhang Y, Royo I, Vilella D, Díez MT, Pelaez F, Ruby C, Kendall RL, Mao X, Griffin P, Calaycay J, Zierath JR, Heck JV, Smith RG, Moller DE
PMID: 10320380 DOI: 10.1126/science.284.5418.1188
Abstract
Insulin elicits a spectrum of biological responses by binding to its cell surface receptor. In a screen for small molecules that activate the human insulin receptor tyrosine kinase, a nonpeptidyl fungal metabolite (L-783,281) was identified that acted as an insulin mimetic in several biochemical and cellular assays. The compound was selective for the insulin receptor versus insulin-like growth factor I (IGF-I) receptor and other receptor tyrosine kinases.

L-783,281 was isolated from an endophytic fungus (Pseudomassaria sp.) and represents a demethylasterriquinone class compound. It directly activates the insulin receptor tyrosine kinase domain, triggering phosphorylation of insulin receptor substrate-1 (IRS-1) and downstream activation of phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase and Akt. The compound also exhibited insulin-like effects on glucose uptake in adipocytes and skeletal muscle tissue ex vivo.

Oral administration of L-783,281 to two mouse models of diabetes (ob/ob and db/db) resulted in significant lowering of blood glucose levels without evidence of acute toxicity. These results demonstrate the feasibility of discovering novel insulin receptor activators that may lead to new oral therapies for diabetes, circumventing the requirement for daily insulin injection in type 1 and some type 2 diabetic patients.
