Insulin fibrillation: toward strategies for attenuating the process
Qafary M, Rashno F, Khajeh K, Khaledi M, Moosavi-Movahedi AA.
Journal of Cellular Physiology · 2021 Mar;236(3):2151-2165
PMID: 32990302
DOI: 10.1002/jcp.29998
Abstract

Dark days for diabetic patients were transformed into an era of hope when the therapeutic usage of insulin was discovered. However, it was subsequently discovered that insulin easily undergoes undesirable, rapid, and irreversible aggregation and fibrillation. After more than half a century of intensive attempts to limit the rate of insulin aggregation and fibrillation, there is no clear-cut strategy for eliminating these processes once and for all.

Insulin is susceptible to fibrillation under conditions of acidic pH and elevated temperature. The native insulin monomer denatures partially so that hydrophobic regions become exposed. Nuclei rich in β-sheets form from partially unfolded monomers, leading to protofibrils and mature amyloid fibrils with characteristic cross-β structure. This process leads to loss of biological activity and can trigger unwanted immune responses in patients receiving insulin therapy.

Insulin fibrillation is inhibited by compounds that either prevent unfolding of the native protein or sequester partially folded, aggregation-competent intermediates. Stabilizing excipients in pharmaceutical formulations—including phenol, zinc ions, polysorbate surfactants, and trehalose—act through multiple mechanisms to suppress hexamer dissociation and monomer unfolding. Analog design also confers differential fibrillation resistance.

This review focuses on various approaches to attenuating and inhibiting insulin aggregation and fibrillation using nanoparticles, proteins/peptides, and cyclic and non-cyclic compounds. Understanding the structural basis for insulin stability is critical for evaluating analog stability, device compatibility for continuous subcutaneous infusion pumps, and the development of insulin formulations suitable for heat-stable delivery in low-resource settings.
