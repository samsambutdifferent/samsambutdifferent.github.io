Identification of a Small Molecular Insulin Receptor Agonist with Potent Antidiabetes Activity

Qiang G, Xue S, Yang JJ, Du G, Pang X, Li X, Goswami D, Griffin PR, Ortlund EA, Chan CB, Ye K
PMID: 24651808 PMCID: PMC3964510 DOI: 10.2337/db13-0334
Abstract
The use of orally bioactive insulin mimetics represents an ideal treatment alternative to insulin injection for diabetes. Here we identify a chaetochromin derivative (4548-G05) that acts as a new nonpeptidyl insulin mimetic. 4548-G05 selectively activates the insulin receptor (IR) but not insulin-like growth factor receptor-I or other receptor tyrosine kinases.

Through binding to the extracellular domain of the IR, 4548-G05 induces activation of the receptor and initiates downstream Akt and extracellular signal-related kinase pathways to trigger glucose uptake in C2C12 myotubes. In vitro binding studies confirmed direct interaction with the insulin receptor ectodomain at a site distinct from the insulin binding interface, consistent with an allosteric mechanism of activation. Quantitative structure-activity relationship analysis of 60 related compounds identified the core pharmacophore required for IR agonist activity.

In vivo, 4548-G05 displays potent blood glucose-lowering effects when administered orally in normal C57BL/6 mice, type 1 diabetic streptozotocin-treated mice, and type 2 diabetic db/db mice — with significant glucose reduction observed within 1–3 hours of dosing. The compound shows no evidence of acute toxicity at effective doses. These results demonstrate that allosteric small molecule activation of the extracellular domain of the insulin receptor is feasible and may provide a viable approach to oral antidiabetic therapy.
