A review of the pharmacological properties of insulin degludec and their clinical relevance
Haahr H, Heise T.
Clinical Pharmacokinetics · 2014 Sep;53(9):787-800
PMID: 25179915
DOI: 10.1007/s40262-014-0165-y
PMCID: PMC4156782
Abstract

Insulin degludec (IDeg) is a new-generation basal insulin with an ultra-long duration of action. It achieves protraction through a novel mechanism: upon subcutaneous injection, IDeg forms soluble multi-hexamer chains in the subcutaneous depot via a fatty acid-mediated self-assembly mechanism distinct from that of insulin glargine, which achieves protraction through isoelectric precipitation.

Data show that IDeg has a half-life of >25 hours (compared with ~12 hours for insulin glargine [IGlar]) and reaches steady state within 3 days of administration in all patient populations investigated. The pharmacokinetic profile of IDeg demonstrates an even distribution of exposure across one dosing interval, with a flat and stable glucose-lowering profile verified by 24-hour euglycaemic clamp studies.

The pharmacodynamic profile of IDeg is flat and stable, demonstrated by an even distribution of glucose-lowering effect across all four 6-hour intervals in a 24-hour period. This profile is distinct from the peak–trough variability observed with NPH insulin and even with insulin detemir. Day-to-day variability in glucose-lowering effect is substantially lower for IDeg than for IGlar.

IDeg's ultra-long action is achieved through fatty acid acylation at LysB29 with a C16 fatty diacid via a glutamic acid linker, enabling reversible albumin binding and very slow release from multi-hexamer chains in the subcutaneous depot. These structural and formulation properties provide the molecular basis for once-daily dosing with reduced risk of nocturnal hypoglycaemia relative to NPH insulin and IGlar.
