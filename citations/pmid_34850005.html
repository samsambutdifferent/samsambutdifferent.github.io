New Horizons: Next-Generation Insulin Analogues: Structural Principles and Clinical Goals
Jarosinski MA, Chen YS, Varas N, Dhayalan B, Chatterjee D, Weiss MA.
Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism · 2022 Mar 24;107(4):909-928
PMID: 34850005
DOI: 10.1210/clinem/dgab849
PMCID: PMC8947325
Abstract

The present year marks both the centennial of insulin's discovery and the golden anniversary of its high-resolution crystal structure. Whereas the former's therapeutic effect was immediate, clinical translation of structural advances has occurred only gradually. Indeed, unmet clinical needs in the treatment of type 1 and type 2 diabetes represent continuing sources of therapeutic innovation.

This review highlights a creative interplay between basic science and molecular pharmacology. Together, a series of advances in diverse technologies—from recombinant expression and protein design to cell biology, omics data, and transgenic model organisms—have enabled and continue to inspire a search for the "perfect insulin." A key theme is the relationship between insulin's evolutionary constraints and the structural features essential for receptor engagement.

The insulin molecule contains conserved structural elements required for receptor binding, including the A-chain helix and specific residues at the receptor-binding surface (IleA2, ValA3, TyrA19, GlyB23, PheB24). These same elements constrain analog design because modifications at the receptor-binding surface typically reduce potency. Next-generation analogs instead exploit regions away from the binding surface—acylation at LysB29 for albumin binding, self-assembly modifications at the B-chain C-terminus for hexamer disruption.

Recent developments have focused on combining genetic and chemical engineering with pharmaceutical optimization to generate ultra-rapid and ultra-long-acting, tissue-selective, or orally delivered insulin analogs. The review further discusses glucose-responsive insulin delivery and proposes that future scientific efforts in molecular engineering should aim to realize the dream of physiologically regulated insulin action.
