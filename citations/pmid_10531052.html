Beta-secretase cleavage of Alzheimer's amyloid precursor protein by the transmembrane aspartic protease BACE
Vassar R, Bennett BD, Babu-Khan S, Kahn S, Mendiaz EA, Denis P, Teplow DB, Ross S, Amarante P, Loeloff R, Luo Y, Fisher S, Fuller J, Edenson S, Lile J, Jarosinski MA, Biere AL, Curran E, Burgess T, Louis JC, Collins F, Treanor J, Rogers G, Citron M.
Science · 1999 Oct 22;286(5440):735-41
PMID: 10531052
DOI: 10.1126/science.286.5440.735
Abstract

Cerebral deposition of amyloid beta peptide (Aβ) is an early and critical feature of Alzheimer's disease (AD). Aβ generation depends on proteolytic cleavage of the amyloid precursor protein (APP) by two unknown proteases: beta-secretase and gamma-secretase.

We have identified a transmembrane aspartic protease, BACE (beta-site APP-cleaving enzyme), as the beta-secretase. BACE has all the known characteristics of beta-secretase: It cleaves APP at the correct sites, is expressed in neurons, and has elevated activity in the brains of patients with AD. Overexpression of BACE in cells caused increased production of the beta-secretase-cleaved form of APP (APPsβ) and of Aβ, whereas inhibitors of aspartic proteases decreased Aβ production.

BACE has an N-terminal signal sequence, a prodomain, two aspartyl protease motifs, a transmembrane domain, and a cytoplasmic tail. It is a type 1 transmembrane protein that localizes predominantly in the Golgi and is also present at the cell surface and in endosomes—subcellular compartments that are the most likely sites of beta-secretase cleavage of APP.

BACE is the first identified transmembrane aspartic protease. Its discovery provides the identity of the long-sought Alzheimer's beta-secretase and a molecular target for AD therapeutic intervention. BACE enzyme represents an attractive drug target for the reduction of cerebral Aβ levels in the treatment or prevention of Alzheimer's disease.
