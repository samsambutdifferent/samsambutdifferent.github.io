Discovery of an Orally Available, Brain Penetrant BACE1 Inhibitor that Affords Robust CNS Aβ Reduction
Stamford AW, Scott JD, Li SW, Babu S, Tadesse D, Hunter R, Wu Y, Misiaszek J, Cumming JN, Gilbert EJ, Huang C, McKittrick BA, Hong L, Guo T, Zhu Z, Strickland C, Orth P, Voigt JH, Kennedy ME, Chen X, Kuvelkar R, Hodgson R, Hyde LA, Cox K, Favreau L, Parker EM, Greenlee WJ.
ACS Medicinal Chemistry Letters · 2012 Nov 8;3(11):897-902
PMID: 23412139
DOI: 10.1021/ml3001165
PMCID: PMC3568987
Abstract

Inhibition of BACE1 to prevent brain Aβ peptide formation is a potential disease-modifying approach to the treatment of Alzheimer's disease. Despite over a decade of drug discovery efforts, the identification of brain-penetrant BACE1 inhibitors that substantially lower CNS Aβ levels following systemic administration remains challenging.

In this report we describe structure-based optimization of a series of brain-penetrant BACE1 inhibitors derived from an iminopyrimidinone scaffold. Application of structure-based design in tandem with control of physicochemical properties culminated in the discovery of compound 16, an iminopyrimidinone that potently reduced cortex and CSF Aβ40 levels when administered orally to rats.

Non-peptidic BACE1 inhibitors offer the most promising avenue to the discovery of orally bioavailable, brain penetrant inhibitors because peptidomimetic inhibitors tend to be P-glycoprotein (Pgp) substrates and suffer restricted brain penetration. The key pharmacophore consists of a non-planar cyclic amidine functionality that forms a network of hydrogen bonds with the catalytic aspartates (Asp32 and Asp228) in the flap-open conformation of BACE1.

Compound 16 potently reduced cortex and CSF Aβ40 levels when administered orally to rats; brain/plasma ratios ranged from 2.4 to 4.2, confirming excellent CNS penetrance. This iminopyrimidinone scaffold represents a promising class for further BACE1 inhibitor development for Alzheimer's disease.
