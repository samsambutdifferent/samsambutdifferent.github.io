Randomized Trial of Verubecestat for Mild-to-Moderate Alzheimer's Disease

Egan MF, Kost J, Tariot PN, Aisen PS, Cummings JL, Vellas B, Sur C, Mukai Y, Voss T, Furtek C, Mahoney E, Harper Mozley L, Vandenberghe R, Mo Y, Michelson D
PMID: 29719179 PMCID: PMC6333738 DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa1706441
Abstract
Background Verubecestat is an orally administered inhibitor of β-site amyloid precursor protein-cleaving enzyme 1 (BACE1) and BACE2 that reduces amyloid-β (Aβ) levels in the cerebrospinal fluid of patients with Alzheimer's disease.
Methods We conducted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate verubecestat at doses of 12 mg and 40 mg per day in patients with mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease. The primary outcome measure was the change from baseline in the score on the Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale — Cognitive subscale (ADAS-Cog). Secondary outcomes included the Clinical Dementia Rating Scale — Sum of Boxes, the Neuropsychiatric Inventory, and other measures of cognition and function.
Results The trial was terminated early for futility after 1,958 patients had been enrolled. Neither dose of verubecestat improved any primary or secondary outcomes versus placebo. Cognitive and functional measures declined similarly across all groups. Adverse events were more common with verubecestat: falls, injuries, sleep disturbance, suicidal ideation, skin hypopigmentation (hair and skin color change), and weight loss occurred at higher rates. Verubecestat reduced Aβ levels in CSF by 63–81%, confirming target engagement, yet produced no clinical benefit.
Conclusions Verubecestat did not benefit patients with mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease and was associated with adverse effects consistent with BACE1 substrate cleavage beyond APP. The trial results do not support BACE1 inhibition as a treatment for established Alzheimer's disease and suggest that intervention timing — before significant amyloid deposition and neuronal loss — may be critical for disease modification.
