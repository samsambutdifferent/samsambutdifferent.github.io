BACE1 (β-Secretase) Inhibitors for the Treatment of Alzheimer's Disease

Vassar R, Kuhn PH, Haass C, Kennedy ME, Rajendran L, Wong PC, Bhaskaran S
PMID: 24691405 DOI: 10.1021/cr500483p
Abstract
BACE1 (β-secretase, memapsin 2, Asp2) has emerged as a promising target for the treatment of Alzheimer's disease. BACE1 is an aspartic protease which functions in the first step of the pathway leading to the production and deposition of amyloid-β peptide (Aβ). Its gene deletion showed only mild phenotypes in mice, suggesting reasonable tolerability of inhibition. BACE1 inhibition has direct implications in the Alzheimer's disease pathology without largely affecting viability. However, inhibiting BACE1 selectively in vivo has presented many challenges to medicinal chemists due to the enzyme's large, open, and flexible active site.

Since its identification in 2000, inhibitors covering many different structural classes have been designed and developed. These inhibitors can be largely classified as either peptidomimetic or non-peptidic inhibitors. Peptidomimetic inhibitors — including hydroxyethylamine (HEA) transition-state mimetics — achieve sub-nanomolar IC50 values by directly occupying the catalytic aspartate dyad. Non-peptidomimetic classes include macrocyclic inhibitors that constrain binding conformation and improve selectivity over BACE2 and cathepsin D, iminopyrimidinones that occupy the S1 and S3 pockets simultaneously, and aminothiazines that engage novel allosteric pockets identified in recent structural studies.

Progress in these fields has resulted in inhibitors that contain many targeted drug-like characteristics suitable for central nervous system penetration. Clinical candidates including verubecestat (MK-8931), atabecestat (JNJ-54861911), lanabecestat (AZD3293), and elenbecestat reached Phase 2–3 trials, all ultimately discontinued for futility or safety concerns. Analysis of failures suggests that selectivity over non-APP BACE1 substrates — particularly neuregulin-1 and sodium channel subunit Scn2b — is critical for tolerability, and that therapeutic window may require intervention at pre-symptomatic disease stages.
