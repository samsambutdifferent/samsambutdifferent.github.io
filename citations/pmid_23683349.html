Inhibitors of BACE for treating Alzheimer's disease: a fragment-based drug discovery story
Stamford A, Strickland C.
Current Opinion in Chemical Biology · 2013 Jun;17(3):320-8
PMID: 23683349
DOI: 10.1016/j.cbpa.2013.04.016
Abstract

Over the past decade, inhibition of BACE1 (β-site amyloid precursor protein cleaving enzyme 1) has attracted enormous interest as a leading approach to the development of disease-modifying Alzheimer's disease (AD) treatments. From the time BACE1 was characterized over a decade ago, inhibition of this enzyme has been considered the preeminent strategy to test the amyloid hypothesis.

BACE1 has proven to be an exceptionally challenging drug discovery target because of the requirement for a BACE1 inhibitor to cross the blood–brain barrier and reach effective intraneuronal concentrations. From the outset, fragment-based approaches have been critical to the discovery of novel inhibitor chemotypes beyond classical peptidomimetic inhibitors, which suffer from poor brain penetration.

Among the most common fragment hits were various amidine-containing molecules in which the amidine engaged in discrete hydrogen bond donor–acceptor interactions with the BACE1 catalytic dyad. Structure and medicinal chemistry knowledge-based optimization with emphasis on ligand efficiency resulted in identification of a key pharmacophore comprising a non-planar cyclic amidine scaffold directly attached to a phenyl group projecting into S1.

This key pharmacophore is a common feature of known clinical candidates and has dominated the recent patent literature. Key elements of successful fragment-based drug discovery approaches targeting BACE1 have been the use of X-ray co-crystal structures to design optimal starting points and an emphasis on ligand efficiency rather than raw affinity to drive the discovery of drug-like, brain-penetrant inhibitors. Multiple fragment-derived BACE1 inhibitors have entered clinical trials.
