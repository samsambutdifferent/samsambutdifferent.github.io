Pharmacological inhibition of BACE1 impairs synaptic plasticity and cognitive functions
Filser S, Ovsepian SV, Masana M, Blazquez-Llorca L, Brandt Elvang A, Volbracht C, Müller MB, Jung CK, Herms J.
Biological Psychiatry · 2015 Apr 15;77(8):729-39
PMID: 25599931
DOI: 10.1016/j.biopsych.2014.10.013
Abstract

Pharmacological inhibition of BACE1 is being pursued as a therapeutic strategy to reduce amyloid-β (Aβ) production in Alzheimer's disease. However, emerging evidence suggests that BACE1 has important physiological substrates beyond APP, raising concerns about mechanism-based side effects of chronic inhibition.

Using two BACE1 inhibitors (SCH1682496 and LY2811376), dose-dependent decreases of Aβ40 levels in vivo confirmed suppression of BACE1 activity. Prolonged treatment caused a reduction in spine formation of layer V pyramidal neurons in the somatosensory cortex, which recovered after withdrawal of inhibitors. These structural changes were accompanied by functional synaptic deficits.

In vivo two-photon microscopy of the somatosensory cortex was used to monitor structural dynamics of dendritic spines, while synaptic functions and plasticity were measured via electrophysiological recordings of excitatory postsynaptic currents and hippocampal long-term potentiation (LTP) in brain slices. The rate of spontaneous and miniature excitatory postsynaptic currents in pyramidal neurons and hippocampal LTP were reduced in BACE1 inhibitor-treated animals.

These effects were not detected in Bace1−/− mice treated with SCH1682496, confirming BACE1 as the pharmacological target. The described structural and functional synaptic changes were associated with cognitive deficits as revealed in behavioral tests. These findings indicate important functions of BACE1 in structural and functional synaptic plasticity in the mature brain, with implications for cognition and the clinical development of BACE1 inhibitors.
